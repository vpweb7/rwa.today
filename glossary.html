RWA Glossary

Real-world asset tokenization comes with a lot of jargon. Here are the key terms explained in plain English.

RWA Tokenization Tokenized Treasury TVL Private Credit Stablecoin Attestation Custodian Redemption KYC Yield On-chain Oracle Permissioned Token Proof of Reserve

Real-World Asset (RWA)

A traditional, off-chain asset — such as a US Treasury bill, a loan, real estate, or gold — represented as a token on a blockchain. The token's value is backed by the underlying asset, which is held by an issuer or custodian.

Tokenization

The process of issuing a blockchain token that represents ownership of, or a claim on, a real-world asset. Tokenization makes the asset transferable, programmable, and tradable on-chain, often around the clock.

Tokenized Treasury

A blockchain token representing a share in a fund that holds short-term US government debt (T-bills). It lets holders earn government-backed yield on-chain. Tokenized treasuries have been one of the fastest-growing RWA categories.

Total Value Locked (TVL)

The total market value of assets held or tracked within a protocol at a given moment. TVL is the most common way to compare the size of RWA and DeFi protocols, though methodologies vary between data providers.

Private Credit

Lending to businesses or individuals outside public bond markets. On-chain private credit protocols originate or fund these loans using blockchain rails, and the loans become the real-world asset backing the tokens.

Stablecoin

A token designed to hold a stable value, usually pegged to a fiat currency like the US dollar. Stablecoins are backed by reserves — often cash and short-term government debt — which makes them, by value, the largest real-world asset class on-chain.

Attestation

A report — often from an independent accounting firm — confirming that the reserves or assets backing a token actually exist as claimed. Regular attestations are a key trust signal for tokenized products.

Custodian

A regulated entity that holds the real-world asset backing a token. The custodian keeps the off-chain asset (cash, bonds, gold) safe while the token representing it circulates on-chain.

Redemption

Exchanging a token back for its underlying asset or cash value. Redeeming typically burns the token (removes it from circulation) and returns the corresponding value to the holder.

KYC (Know Your Customer)

Identity-verification checks that many regulated RWA products require before an investor can mint, hold, or redeem tokens. KYC is part of what distinguishes institutional RWA products from permissionless crypto tokens.

Yield

The return earned on an asset, usually expressed as an annual percentage. For tokenized treasuries, the yield reflects the interest paid by the underlying government debt; for private credit, it reflects the loan interest.

On-chain

Recorded and executed directly on a blockchain, as opposed to "off-chain" systems like traditional bank ledgers. RWA tokenization bridges off-chain assets into on-chain markets.

Oracle

A service that delivers external data — such as asset prices or proof of reserves — onto a blockchain, so smart contracts can act on real-world information. Oracles are critical infrastructure for RWA protocols.

Permissioned Token

A token that can only be held or transferred by approved, identity-verified wallets. Many regulated RWA products use permissioned tokens to comply with securities rules.

Proof of Reserve

On-chain or independently verified evidence that the assets backing a token exist and match the number of tokens issued. It helps holders confirm a token is fully backed.
